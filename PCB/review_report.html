Board Overview

Platform: RC Radio Transmitter controller based on STM32H745ZIT6 dual-core Arm Cortex-M7/M4.

Key Interfaces: USB-C (USB 2.0 FS), 2× ELRS RF module connectors, 2× Joystick connectors (6-pin), 2× Encoder connectors (8-pin), Analog potentiometer connector, Digital potentiometer connector, SWD programming header, Battery power input.

Power System: Battery input → reverse-polarity protection (AO3407A P-FET + D1 Schottky) → VCC rail → 4× AP2112K-3.3 LDO (600 mA each) → 3.3 V rails (LD1–LD4). STM32H745 internal SMPS (L1 = 2.2 µH) used for core supply.

EMC: DLW21SN900SQ2L CMC on USB data lines, BLM18AG102SN1D ferrite bead on USB/analog power, 7× SRV05-4 TVS arrays on I/O.

Power Architecture Review

LOW AP2112K-3.3 LDO Dropout at End-of-Discharge — Acts as Implicit Low-Battery Protection LD1–LD4

The AP2112K-3.3 has a worst-case dropout voltage of 300–600 mV at 600 mA, meaning VIN must be ≥ 3.6 V to maintain regulated 3.3 V output. A 1S LiPo discharges from 4.2 V to a hard protection cut-off of ~3.0 V. Below VIN = 3.6 V the LDO output tracks VIN − VDO — the MCU will brownout-reset at approximately VCC ≈ 3.0–3.3 V, which is well above the battery’s 3.0 V over-discharge threshold.

This behaviour acts as implicit low-battery protection: the transmitter will fail-safe (MCU resets) before the battery is deeply discharged. No hardware change is required. Note: the KiCad symbol description states “3.8V–6V input voltage range” — the actual datasheet VIN(min) is 2.5 V; the 3.8 V figure is a worst-case start-up guideline from an application note (see also L1).

Recommended firmware enhancement: Monitor VCC via an MCU ADC input (resistor divider from VCC to a 3.3 V ADC-safe level) and alert the user — LED, display, or telemetry — when VCC drops below ~3.7 V. This provides advance warning before LDO dropout, preventing unexpected mid-flight power loss. Ensure any NVM or flash write operations complete before VCC falls to 3.3 V to avoid data corruption.
HIGH USB Differential Pair Impedance Mismatch — 0.35 mm Traces Give ~140 Ω on 0.421 mm Prepreg J1, L2, R52, R53

The USB_DIFF netclass defines trace width 0.35 mm and gap 0.18 mm. Calculating edge-coupled microstrip impedance for F.Cu routing above L2_GND (H = 0.421 mm, εr = 4.4, T = 35 µm):

  Z0 (single-ended, W = 0.35 mm) ≈ 78 Ω

  Zdiff (edge-coupled, S = 0.18 mm) ≈ 140 Ω

USB 2.0 requires Zdiff = 90 Ω ±15%. The current routing is approximately 55% too high. To achieve 90 Ω differential on this layer, the trace width must be approximately 0.80 mm (with gap 0.18 mm). The KiCad DRC "unable to find complementary differential pair" warning also suggests the diff pair routing is not correctly coupled.

Action: Correct the USB_DIFF netclass track width to 0.80 mm (or use an impedance calculator for the exact stackup). Re-route J1 USB P/N traces and verify coupling from the connector to the common-mode choke (L2) and series resistors (R52, R53 = 22 Ω). If board space is too tight for 0.8 mm traces near J1, consider routing USB through an inner signal layer between two closely spaced GND planes (L5 between L4_GND and L6_GND at 0.138 mm spacing, which would require much narrower traces but is impractical near the edge connector). Alternatively, accept that this is USB 2.0 Full-Speed (12 Mbps) only and verify by USB compliance testing, since FS is less sensitive to impedance than HS.
MEDIUM SMPS Inductor L1 — Saturation & DCR Specification Unverified L1, LCSC C48945021

L1 (2.2 µH, 0603) is used in the STM32H745 internal SMPS loop (VLXSMPS ↔ VDDSMPS). LCSC part C48945021 is specified as 2.2 µH, which matches ST’s recommendation in AN5188. However, the SMPS output current for core supply can reach 100–200 mA peaks. The inductor must be rated for: saturation current ≥ 300 mA, DCR ≤ 300 mΩ (to limit efficiency loss), and self-resonance frequency well above 2.1 MHz switching frequency.

The specific part number for LCSC C48945021 should be confirmed against its datasheet (rated Isat and DCR are not visible in the schematic fields).

Action: Verify that LCSC C48945021 has Isat ≥ 300 mA and DCR ≤ 300 mΩ. Per ST AN5188, the SMPS inductor placement is also critical — the loop (VLXSMPS, VDDSMPS, VSSSMPS, VFBSMPS) must be as compact as possible with a local 1 µF + 10 nF bypass at VDDSMPS. Confirm these capacitors exist and are placed immediately adjacent to the MCU.
PASS USB-C VDD50USB / VDD33USB Bypass Mode — Valid STM32H745, /LD1_3.3v

Both VDD50USB and VDD33USB are tied to the /LD1_3.3v (3.3 V) rail. Per STM32H745 datasheet §5.1.7, when the application operates entirely at 3.3 V and does not require the 5 V USB transceiver internal supply, VDD50USB may be connected to VDD33USB (both at 3.3 V) in “bypass mode.” This is a valid and documented configuration for USB 2.0 operation.

Action: None — confirmed valid. No hardware change required.
PASS USB-C CC Resistors (5.1 kΩ) — Correct for UFP/Device Mode R41, R42

R41 and R42 = 5.1 kΩ are the CC1/CC2 pull-down (Rd) resistors required by the USB-C specification for an Upstream-Facing Port (device). The USB PD specification mandates Rd = 5.1 kΩ ±10% for device identification. This is correct.

Action: None — correct implementation.
PASS Reverse Polarity Protection — AO3407A P-FET + Schottky D1 U1, D1, POWER_1

AO3407A (P-channel MOSFET, SOT-23-3, Vds = −20 V, Id = −4 A) with a Schottky diode (D1, LCSC C8598, ~SS14) provides low-loss reverse polarity protection on the battery input. The gate drive for U1 is typical for this topology (VBATT on source, GND through gate resistor or direct). This is a well-established approach for LiPo-powered portable devices.

Action: None — correct design.
PASS 4× Independent LDOs for Domain Isolation LD1–LD4

Using four separate AP2112K-3.3 regulators (each 600 mA) to create independent 3.3 V domains (USB/IO, MCU VDD, ELRS, analog) is good practice. This prevents noise coupling between high-current switching loads (ELRS RF) and sensitive analog circuitry. Each domain should have its LDO output capacitor and ferrite bead physically close to the regulator.

Action: Confirm physical placement of each LDO relative to its load domain in PCB layout review.

MCU Circuitry Review — STM32H745ZIT6

PASS Crystal Load Capacitors — 12 pF Correct for TSX-3225 24 MHz Y1, C23, C24

TSX-3225 24.0000MF20G-AC3 has a specified load capacitance of CL = 8 pF. Using two 12 pF load capacitors (C23, C24): CL(circuit) = (C1 × C2)/(C1 + C2) + Cstray = (12 × 12)/(24) + ~2 pF = 6 + 2 = 8 pF ✓. The 220 pF capacitor C61 appears to be a CL decoupling aid for the crystal oscillator circuit. Both C23 and C24 use LCSC C882527 (12 pF, 0603, C0G/NP0) — the correct low-tempco dielectric for crystal load capacitors.

Action: None — correct implementation.
PASS VCAP Capacitors Present for STM32H745 Internal LDO VCAP pins, 2.2 µF caps

STM32H745 requires 2.2 µF (minimum) capacitors on VCAP1 (pin 75) and VCAP2 (pin 99), connected directly to GND. Multiple 2.2 µF capacitors (LCSC C23630, 0603) are present in the BOM. These are the internal voltage regulator stabilisation capacitors and are required for proper CPU operation.

Action: Verify in layout that VCAP capacitors are placed within 0.5 mm of the MCU pads with direct GND via connections.
PASS MCU Decoupling Capacitor Network — Well-Defined C3–C62 (various)

The BOM shows a comprehensive decoupling network: 100 nF (LCSC C24452) bulk bypass capacitors, 10 nF (C3836718) high-frequency bypass, 4.7 µF (C19666) bulk capacitors, and 1 µF (C2177011) mid-frequency bypass. This multi-tier bypassing strategy is appropriate for an LQFP-144 MCU at frequencies up to 480 MHz (STM32H745 max CPU clock).

Action: Ensure bypass capacitors are evenly distributed around all four sides of the LQFP-144 package in layout.
MEDIUM BOOT0 Pin Configuration — Pull-Down Resistor Value to Verify BOOT0, R1/R2

The STM32H745 BOOT0 pin must be pulled LOW (to GND) for normal flash boot. R1 and R2 are both 10 kΩ, which appear to be used for BOOT0 control (possibly a pull-down to GND with optional pull-up via programming connector). The STM32H745 internal BOOT0 pull-down is approximately 40 kΩ (weak), so an external pull-down of 10 kΩ is appropriate. Verify the net connectivity ensures BOOT0 is de-asserted during normal power-up.

Action: Confirm that BOOT0 is connected through R1/R2 such that it defaults LOW (GND) at power-on. Ensure the PROGRAMMING_1 connector pins are clearly labelled and that a test plan verifies booting into user flash after programming.

Bill of Materials Review

HIGH 10 nF Capacitor LCSC Part Number Conflict Between Netlist and CSV BOM C19, C25–C28, C32–C37, C45

The exported CSV BOM groups C19, C25, C26, C27, C28, C32, C33, C34, C35, C36, C37, C45 as 10 nF with LCSC C3836718. However, the netlist (generated 2026-05-31) assigns LCSC C24452 (a 100 nF part) to these same components, while retaining the value field as "10nF". This creates an ordering conflict: purchasing C24452 will yield 100 nF capacitors placed in positions intended for 10 nF.

This discrepancy suggests either (a) the LCSC field was accidentally changed to C24452 for these components without updating the value, or (b) the CSV BOM is stale and the designer intentionally changed them to 100 nF. In either case, the two sources are inconsistent and must be resolved before ordering.

Action: Open the schematic and verify the intended value for C19, C25–C28, C32–C37, C45. If 10 nF: correct the LCSC number to C3836718. If 100 nF: update the value field. Then re-export the BOM and ensure the netlist LCSC field matches. Do not order parts until this is reconciled.
LOW AP2112K-3.3 Symbol Description Overstates Minimum Input Voltage LD1–LD4

The KiCad library description for AP2112K-3.3 states "3.8V–6V input voltage range". The actual AP2112K datasheet (Diodes Inc.) specifies VIN(min) = 2.5 V (absolute minimum) and VOUT(min) + Vdropout as the functional minimum, which is approximately 3.6 V at full load for 3.3 V output. The "3.8V" figure in the library description is a typical worst-case start-up voltage from an older application note and is not an absolute minimum. This mismatch could cause confusion during design review but does not affect function.

Action: Update the KiCad symbol description field to "VIN 2.5V–6V, Vdropout typ 300mV @ 600mA" for clarity. This is a documentation issue only.
LOW Test Points TP1/TP2 Have No LCSC Numbers TP1, TP2

TP1 and TP2 are listed in the BOM without LCSC part numbers. If these are copper pad test points (no physical component), they should be marked DNP (Do Not Place) in the BOM to avoid assembly errors. If they use solderable test point pins, a part number is required.

Action: Mark TP1/TP2 as DNP in the schematic BOM fields if they are bare pad test points. Otherwise, assign the appropriate LCSC part number.

MEDIUM USB-C Connector Edge Clearance Exception — Fragility Risk J1, DRC rule edge_J1_exception

A custom DRC rule permits J1 (USB-C) pads to be only 0.1 mm from the board edge, compared to the standard 0.3 mm edge clearance rule. The HRO-TYPE-C-31-M-13x is an edge-mount USB-C receptacle, so some pads will naturally approach the board edge. However, 0.1 mm clearance to the milled edge is extremely tight. Most PCB fabs quote a ±0.1 mm router tolerance, meaning the copper could be exposed or cut in a worst-case scenario.

Action: Confirm with your target fab (JLCPCB / PCBWay) that their edge routing tolerance and their minimum copper-to-edge for edge-mount connectors allows 0.1 mm. Consider adding a copper-free keepout around the J1 footprint edge pads if the fab requires 0.15 mm or larger clearance. Verify with the gerber viewer that edge pads are not overlapping the board outline.
PASS 8-Layer Stackup Provides Excellent Shielding and Power Integrity

Four dedicated GND planes (L2, L4, L6, L8) ensure every signal layer (L1, L5, L7) and the power plane (L3) has an adjacent GND reference, minimising return path inductance. The ELRS RF signals are isolated on L5 (between L4-GND and L6-GND), providing natural shielding for sensitive RF traces. The analog layer (L7) is adjacent to the bottom GND (L8), ideal for low-noise ADC signal routing.

Action: None — excellent layer assignment strategy.
PASS Teardrops Enabled on All Pad Types

Teardrops are enabled for PTH pads, SMD pads, and vias (as seen in .kicad_pro). Teardrops improve mechanical reliability at the pad-to-trace junction and reduce acid traps during etching, which is particularly important for the fine-pitch LQFP-144 STM32 pads.

Action: None — good DFM practice.
LOW DRC Diff Pair Rule Gap Limits May Not Match Actual Routing .kicad_dru, USB_DIFF netclass

The DRU specifies USB_DIFF diff pair gap min 0.10 mm, max 0.20 mm. The netclass in .kicad_pro shows diff_pair_dimensions with gap = 0 and width = 0 (default/placeholder values). If the diff pair was not routed using KiCad’s interactive diff pair router, these constraints will not have been enforced during routing, and the DRC “unable to find complementary diff pair” warning confirms this.

Action: After correcting the track width to ~0.80 mm (see HIGH finding), re-route using the KiCad interactive diff pair router so that gap constraints are automatically enforced. Set the diff_pair_dimensions entry with explicit gap (0.18 mm) and width (0.80 mm) values so DRC can validate the result.
LOW Gerber Job File Missing Surface Finish and PCB Revision Orthrus-TX-job.gbrjob

The gerber job file lists "Finish": "None" and the PCB revision field reads "rev?". For fabrication submission, the finish must be specified (HASL, ENIG, OSP, etc.) and a proper revision identifier should be filled in. ENIG is recommended for the fine-pitch LQFP-144 and 0.5 mm FPC connector footprints to ensure reliable soldering.

Action: Set the board finish in KiCad Board Setup → Board Stackup to “ENIG” (or intended finish) and update the revision field before final gerber export.

EMC / Signal Integrity Review

PASS USB Common-Mode Filtering — DLW21SN900SQ2L + BLM18AG102SN1D L2, L3, J1

DLW21SN900SQ2L is a 2-line common-mode choke (90 Ω @ 100 MHz, 0805) on the USB D+/D− lines — the correct component for suppressing common-mode interference on USB 2.0. BLM18AG102SN1D (1 kΩ @ 100 MHz ferrite bead) on the USB VBUS/power supply rail further reduces conducted EMI into the MCU. This is a textbook USB EMI filter topology.

Action: Ensure L2 is placed as close as possible to J1 (within 5 mm), before the 22 Ω series resistors R52/R53. Filter placement order from connector: J1 → L2 (CMC) → L3 (ferrite, VBUS) → R52/R53 → STM32.
PASS 7× SRV05-4 ESD Arrays for I/O Protection U2–U8

Seven SRV05-4 (5-line TVS diode array, SOT-23-6, 5 V clamping) instances provide ESD protection on connector I/O lines. This is good practice for an RC transmitter that interfaces with external modules (ELRS, joysticks, encoders) which may be hot-plugged in the field. SRV05-4 has a typical capacitance of ~1 pF per line, which is negligible at the signal speeds used.

Action: Ensure ESD devices are placed between the connector and the MCU/internal circuitry (not after), with short traces to a local via stitched to GND.
PASS ELRS RF Isolation — Dedicated Layer L5 with GND Sandwich

ELRS (ExpressLRS) RF module signals are routed on L5 (In4.Cu), sandwiched between L4_GND and L6_GND. This provides excellent shielding for 900 MHz / 2.4 GHz RF traces and reduces coupling into the analog and digital domains. Dedicated GND layers above and below the RF signal layer is the correct approach for RF PCB design.

Action: Verify that stitching vias connect L4_GND to L6_GND around the ELRS connector area to form a Faraday cage effect for the RF traces.
PASS Analog Domain Separation — Dedicated Layer L7 with GND Reference

Joystick potentiometer signals and ADC inputs are routed on L7 (In6.Cu — ANALOG), adjacent to the L8 bottom GND plane. This gives a clean reference for analog signals and minimises coupling from switching digital signals. The 10 kΩ pull-up resistors on ADC input lines (R3–R12, 100 Ω series damping) further protect the MCU ADC inputs.

Action: Ensure the LDO domain supplying the MCU VDDA pin routes through L3_POWER and that the VDDA bypass capacitors are not shared with noisy digital supplies.
